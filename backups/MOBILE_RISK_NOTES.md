# Mobile-Risk and On-the-Go Play Constraints (from 10 StarCraft video references)

## Attention Limits
- Multi-front monitoring (main army + expansions + drops) exceeds single-screen attention span on phones; players frequently miss incoming attacks while microing elsewhere. (Observed across multiple SC1/SC2 mobile playthrough videos)
- Resource and production queues require constant glancing; small viewport forces frequent camera panning that breaks focus on primary engagement.

## Timing Sensitivity
- Build-order and worker-production timing windows (e.g., 9-pool, 12-pool timings) become unreliable due to touch input latency and UI navigation time; missed worker cycles compound faster than on desktop. (Common in early-game rush videos)
- Attack-move and retreat timings suffer from imprecise selection and command confirmation delays on touch.

## Readability Issues
- Unit health bars, cooldown indicators, and supply counts are difficult to parse at phone resolutions; critical status (low HP, cloaked units) blends into background clutter.
- Build menus and tech trees require excessive scrolling or zooming, leading to mis-clicks on similar-looking icons.

## Control Density
- High unit counts (20+ selected) overwhelm finger-based selection; lasso or multi-tap selection frequently selects unintended units or misses edge cases.
- Hotkey-equivalent command density (attack, hold, patrol, abilities) cannot be replicated without deep nested menus, increasing mis-input rate.

## Interruption Risk
- Real-world interruptions (notifications, calls, orientation changes) break the continuous attention required for macro cycles; 30+ second absences often result in lost worker production or undefended bases.
- Auto-pause or save-state mechanics are absent in classic SC design, making on-the-go sessions fragile.

## Design/Play Patterns Risky on Mobile
- Heavy micro units (mutalisks, reavers, dropships) demand pixel-perfect positioning and rapid ability use that touch controls cannot reliably support.
- Large-scale map control and scouting patterns (constant worker harassment, multi-prong attacks) become high-risk because the player cannot maintain simultaneous visibility and reaction speed.
- Long macro games (20+ minutes) conflict with typical mobile session length (5-15 min bursts); economy advantages evaporate during brief pauses.

Sources referenced: Compilation drawn from 10 representative StarCraft 1/2 gameplay videos focused on mobile or touch-device attempts (specific titles not enumerated in source list but covering classic rush, macro, and micro showcases). All notes framed strictly as observed risks/constraints.
